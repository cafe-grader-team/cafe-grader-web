# Student Guide: Taking a Viva Exam

A **viva** is a spoken-style exam done as a text conversation instead of a
code submission. You'll read a short scenario, then answer questions about
it from an AI examiner. When the examiner has enough to go on, the
interview ends and you get a score with written feedback.

## The ground rules

- Answer the examiner's questions honestly, in your own words.
- If you're genuinely confused or don't know something, say so. That's
  normal and is never penalized on its own — the examiner is trying to find
  out what you understand, not trap you.
- Don't try to get the examiner to reveal the rubric, the correct answers,
  or your score mid-interview, and don't try to get it to act as if it were
  you, an instructor, or anything other than the examiner. The examiner is
  built to notice attempts like this, decline politely, and flag them for
  your instructor to review.

## Practice limits and restarting

You can usually start a fresh attempt a limited number of times per day
(shown on the viva page). You can restart your own viva between sessions at
any time — this sets your current attempt aside and lets you begin again,
still counted against your daily limit.

## Your score is your best attempt

Restarting never lowers your score. Your final grade for a viva is the
highest score across every attempt you've made, including ones you
restarted away from. There's no downside to trying again if you think you
can do better.

## Transcripts are kept

Every conversation you have — your current attempt or an older one you set
aside — is saved in full, and your instructor can review it at any time.

## What the warning notice means

If your session shows a flag on a turn, it means the examiner noticed
something that looked like an attempt to get around the exam rules — for
example, asking it to just give you the answer. It doesn't stop your
interview; you can keep going. But it is visible to your instructor for
review.
